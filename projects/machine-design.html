Machine design: control-rod module assembly machine

University of Bath · Machine Design (Year 2) · 2023 – 24

Context

The client needed a machine that takes control rods for a nuclear reactor from a 500 mm conveyor, in no particular order, and packs them into two carriers to form a module, running 24 hours a day for up to 10 years.

What I did

  • Chose the concept with a morphological chart: the rods are funnelled into a feed, scanned and sorted by orientation, then split across two levels by 13 splitters into their positions.
  • Selected and integrated off-the-shelf components, then ran FEA to confirm that the critical parts could carry their loads.
  • Produced a full CAD model and a set of engineering drawings.
Role
Team leader, team of 4
Result
71% (First). The concept assembles a module in 136.4 s against a 180 s target, for £20,746 excluding labour.
