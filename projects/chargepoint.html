ChargePoint Technology: design placement

ChargePoint Technology, Liverpool, UK · Design Engineer (placement) · Aug 2024 – Feb 2025

Context

ChargePoint makes containment and sterile-transfer valves for pharmaceutical manufacturing. I worked in SolidWorks across six projects.

What I learned

My first professional role taught me how an engineering company works and how to conduct myself in it. I learnt to plan and run engineering experiments and product tests, picked up practical details such as sizing O-ring grooves, and wrote my first real Excel code.

Role
Design Engineer
Result
Sensor box size halved; a company-wide product database delivered in one month against a six-month plan.

Reduced-footprint sensor box

A customer's plant could not fit the standard sensor box. I designed 150 mm, 200 mm and single-sensor versions, then noticed the sensors could sit on one plane, which halved the size of the box. My favourite ChargePoint project.

Wash-system test frame for a 100 kg product

My first engineering task: a 316 stainless steel frame holding an experimental 100 kg product on an IBC. I sized off-the-shelf beams with beam theory and checked them with FEA, and learnt welding symbols, warping and dimensioning for technicians.

Valve lifting points

Fully equipped valves weigh over 100 kg. I wrote a test report using bolt shear analysis, then designed a handle variant for an eyebolt and checked its pull-out strength. The lifting-eye connector was later used for handles a customer requested.

Compensator variant with steel springs

I calculated the spring rate and selected the springs, worked with the ChargeBag (plastics) engineers on a sealed spring cover and its material, learnt O-ring groove design and wrote the test procedures.

PuroGrab vibratory attachment

Powder was blocking PuroVaso bottle necks, and users were hitting the bottles with hammers to clear them. I designed a bracket that fits a vibratory piston to the PuroGrab holder, following the company's new-product procedure, and sent the drawings for approval before leaving.

Product database automation

Product information was spread over many sources. I noticed that the product codes encode each specification, and taught myself Excel functions to decode every code against a master sheet and fill the fields automatically, including for future products. The first version was ready in one month against a six-month plan.
