Final-year project: serrated wingtip devices

University of Bath · Final Year Project · Supervisor: Dr Zhijing Wang · 2026 – 2027

Context

Air leaking round a wingtip from the high-pressure underside to the low-pressure top forms tip vortices and lift-induced drag, a large share of an airliner's drag. Winglets reduce this, saving fuel and CO2. My project asks whether sine-wave serrations on the edges of an A350-style winglet can weaken the tip vortex, which matters for wake hazard and aircraft spacing, and reduce drag without losing lift.

What I did

  • Proposed the project myself.
  • Chose the variables: serrations on the top edge, the trailing edge and both, varying the sine wave's amplitude and frequency over a range of angles of attack.
  • Chose the A350 wingtip device as the baseline, and a test approach with interchangeable tips on one wind-tunnel model, supported by Ansys CFD.
  • Reviewing the literature on wingtip devices and serrated edges.
Role
Individual project, self-initiated
Result
In progress. Literature review under way; CAD, CFD and wind-tunnel testing planned. No results yet.
Skills
Experimental designAerodynamicsLiterature reviewResearch proposal

Decisions so far

I defined the serrations as sine waves set by amplitude and frequency: two continuous variables that make trends easy to see, and the closest published study found wavelength mattered more than waveform shape. I measure vortex strength and drag as separate outcomes, because a weaker vortex does not automatically mean less drag. And every tip fits the same model, so the wing and mounting stay identical and small drag differences come from the tip, not the set-up.
